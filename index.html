I am a master’s student in Computer Science at Binghamton University, working in the Autonomous Intelligent Robotics (AIR) Lab under the supervision of Prof. Shiqi Zhang. My research focuses on robot learning for humanoid locomotion and manipulation.

My long-term goal is general-purpose humanoid loco-manipulation: robots that can move through their surroundings and interact with objects. I currently build a demonstration-learning pipeline that connects vision-based teleoperation, demonstration data, imitation learning, and autonomous policy execution. This work provides a foundation for learning tasks such as grasping, carrying, and relocating objects.

Before graduate research, I spent eight years building production software and large-scale data platforms, including four years at PayPal. I bring software engineering, distributed systems, and production reliability experience to robotics research.

Current Research

Towards Humanoid Loco-Manipulation through Learning from Demonstration

Ongoing research · 2026–Present

I connect visual teleoperation data with imitation learning and whole-body control. The current system supports human demonstration collection, data inspection and preparation, policy training, and execution with robot feedback. The goal is to use this workflow to learn increasingly complex tasks involving both movement and object manipulation.

Research poster (PDF) Learning pipeline

Demonstration-to-Policy Learning Pipeline

Current takeaway. Visual teleoperation data, imitation learning, and robot control are connected in one workflow. Initial simulation results demonstrate autonomous target interaction, providing a starting point for more complex loco-manipulation tasks.

Poster authors: Xiaopeng Wu, Austine Oloo, Zainab Altaweel, Xiaohan Zhang, and Shiqi Zhang.
